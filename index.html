Manipulated Realities: Deceptive Design in AI-Powered Extended Reality

Deceptive design patterns are already widespread on the web, on mobile, and across social media, but Extended Reality raises the stakes even higher. XR can alter perception, redirect physical movement, and shape choices in real time, all while drawing on rich sensing data about people and their surroundings. Our workshop Manipulated Realities brings researchers, designers, policy experts, and industry together to chart how such harms can be identified, evaluated, prevented, and mitigated.

Co-located with CHI 2027 · Pittsburgh, USA · half-day, in person

01 Abstract

Extended Reality (XR) is evolving into AI-powered spatial computing that combines immersive interfaces, continuous sensing of users and environments, personalization, generative content, and intelligent agents. However, these developments enable new forms of deception and manipulation. XR systems can alter perception, redirect movement, infer interests and emotions from gaze and physiological signals, and deploy persuasive agents tailored to individual vulnerabilities. Although deceptive design research has developed taxonomies, measurement methods, and regulatory insights for web and mobile interfaces, it remains unclear how these approaches transfer to AI-powered immersive systems. To understand and act on these challenges, our workshop explores how generative AI can accelerate the development of deceptive XR designs by replicating manipulative patterns, creating persuasive content, and customizing deceptive experiences for users, contexts, and physical environments. Together, we will build a shared understanding of deceptive mechanisms, identify methodological gaps, and formulate a research agenda to safeguard user autonomy in XR systems.

03 Call for Participation

Deceptive design patterns, meaning interface and interaction techniques that steer people toward choices against their own interests, are widespread in web, mobile, e-commerce, and social media interfaces. Extended Reality introduces new risks because it can alter perception, manipulate physical movement, and shape user choices while relying on rich sensing data from users and their environments.

This workshop invites researchers, practitioners, designers, policy experts, and industry stakeholders to examine how deceptive design manifests in XR and how we can identify, evaluate, prevent, and mitigate such harms. Manipulated realities addresses the critical challenge of how generative AI may accelerate deceptive XR design by reproducing manipulative patterns, generating persuasive content, and tailoring deceptive experiences to individual users, contexts, preferences, vulnerabilities, and physical surroundings.

The workshop combines short lightning talks, paper and demo presentations, and an interactive design session. We will bring together experts to build a shared research agenda for responsible XR before deceptive design patterns become normalized in XR ecosystems.

Topics of Interest

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • deceptive design patterns in augmented, mixed, virtual, and extended reality;
  • manipulation of human perception within XR;
  • AI-generated or AI-personalized deceptive designs;
  • privacy, security, and safety in deceptive XR systems;
  • social deception and interpersonal manipulation in XR;
  • empirical methods for measuring the impact of deceptive XR designs;
  • factors affecting user susceptibility and resilience in deceptive XR;
  • detection tools, transparency mechanisms, and mitigation strategies;
  • ethical, legal, and regulatory perspectives on deceptive XR;
  • best practices for responsible XR design; and
  • demonstrations and prototypes of deceptive XR.

Submission Types

We welcome three types of contributions. All submissions should follow the ACM two-column template and be 2–4 pages long, excluding references.

  • Position papers
  • Research statements
  • Demonstrations

Contributions are selected based on relevance, originality, and their potential to stimulate discussion. We encourage authors to make their work available on arXiv after the workshop. At least one author of each accepted submission must attend the workshop. The workshop is open to all CHI attendees, and all participants must register.
